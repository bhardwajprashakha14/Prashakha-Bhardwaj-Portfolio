Case study 03 Healthcare · Self initiated concept

Jaanch: lab tests at home, without the guesswork

Most apps make booking a home lab test quick. The confusion starts afterwards. Jaanch is a concept that designs for everything after the booking: preparation, tracking each test, and recovering when a sample fails.

RoleSolo: research to design system
SurfacesApp, web, ops console, collector app
Timeline4 weeks, October 2026
StatusConcept
Reading timeAbout 5 min

NoteSelf-initiated concept with no client. Not yet tested with users; the planned study is in section 07.

Project at a glance

Problem
Booking a home lab test is already easy. The hard parts come after: matching a prescription to the right test, preparing correctly, following each test, and recovering when a sample can't be used.
My contribution
Solo, end to end: desk research, proto-personas, service flows, four connected apps, 68 screens in light and dark, and a 30 component design system.
Key decision
Design around what happens after booking: prep rules before the slot, a status for every test, and an owner for every failed sample.
Validation status
A moderated usability study with patients and ops staff is planned; see section 07.

01 Context

Booking works. Everything after it needs help.

Patient app

Book, prepare and track

Pick tests, see prep rules before choosing a slot, follow each test.

The same booking on a bigger screen

The booking summary stays on screen while you fill in details.

Who needs to act

Collector app

Verify, scan and hand over

Built to keep working without a connection at the door.

02 Evidence

What desk research showed, and what I assumed.

Desk research

Booking is already easy

Big providers already let people search, book and download reports. That alone won't make anyone switch.

Desk research

Test names are confusing

Prescriptions say TFT or FBS, or are handwritten, and people can't match them to what an app lists.

Desk research

Prep decides the result

Fasting tests need 8 to 12 hours without food, so a 6 PM slot for a fasting test doesn't work.

I wrote two proto-personas as assumptions to test: Meera, who books for herself and her father, and Anjali, a coordinator who hears about failed samples too late.

See the proto-personas and journey map
03 Strategy

Three ideas guided every screen.

Idea

Prep before the slot

If fasting decides the time, people see the rule before they pick a slot.

Idea

A status per test

A finished blood count is shown right away, even if another test is still running.

Idea

Every failure has an owner

A failed sample is assigned to a named person in the ops console until a re-visit is booked.

04 Recovery

When a sample fails, someone owns it.

The riskiest moment is telling someone their sample can't be used. I mapped it across the lab, the system, the coordinator and the patient before designing a single screen.

Qualified

"Two taps"

It takes two taps only if the suggested slot works. Choosing another slot adds steps.

Assumption

A free re-visit

"This visit is on us" is a business policy I assumed. A real lab would need to agree to it.

Design iteration Found in design review, not in user testing

Before

“The blood clotted in the tube before the lab could test it. This happens sometimes and has nothing to do with your health.”

After

“We couldn’t complete your fasting sugar test with the sample collected. Please book another collection.”

The app can't know that a failed sample has nothing to do with someone's health, so the first version made a promise it couldn't keep. The revised copy says only what is known: which test couldn't be completed and what to do next. A specific cause appears only when the lab records one, as in the ops console. The screens above use the revised copy.

05 Operations

The ops console and the collector app carry the same booking.

Offline

Saved on this device

The collector sees how many entries are waiting to sync.

Offline

Sync failed

A clear retry, with the promise that nothing will be entered twice.

Ownership

One name per exception

Coordinators see who owns each problem instead of a shared inbox.

06 Patient screens

Booking, tracking and the website.

Design system, dark mode and edge states

Tokens for colour, type, spacing and radius in light and dark themes. Every status badge has a word as well as a colour.

07 Validation plan

How I'd validate it.

Planned, not run
Planned study

Written and ready to run. No sessions have taken place yet.

Who

The people most likely to struggle

Someone over 60, a first-time health app user, someone booking for a parent, and partner lab coordinators.

First question

Can people find the right test?

Starting from a real prescription, without knowing the medical name.

Second question

Is the failure message clear?

Whether people understand what happened and feel able to rebook.

Measure

Task completion

Did they finish booking and rebooking?

Measure

Time to book a re-visit

From the failure notice to a confirmed slot.

Measure

Prep errors

Did they pick a slot that conflicts with fasting?

08 Reflection

The booking is the easy part. The design work is in what follows.

Next, I would run the study above and talk to a partner lab about whether free re-visits and named owners fit how they work.
