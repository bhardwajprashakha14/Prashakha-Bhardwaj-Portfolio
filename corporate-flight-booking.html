Case study 04 Corporate travel design challenge

Policy aware corporate flight booking

Booking a flight for work is different from booking a holiday. The cheapest or fastest flight can still be the wrong choice if it breaks company policy, needs approval, or uses a route the traveller's visa doesn't cover. This flow shows those risks on each flight while people compare options.

01 The challenge

One traveller, one trip, five imperfect options.

The brief asked for four screens for an international work trip: search results, flight details, traveller details, and review and approval. They had to work on desktop and mobile, be built from components a developer could use directly, and include one new idea of my own.

Riya, a senior account manager, needs to fly from Delhi to London for a week of client work. She is booking 12 days ahead, but the policy asks for 14. That means every option needs approval, even the ones that follow every other rule.

02 The problem

Typical booking flows hide what matters for business travel.

Problem 1

Policy shows up too late

Approval needs usually appear at checkout, after the traveller has already chosen.

Problem 2

Different risks look the same

A policy exception, a long layover and a missing transit visa are different problems, yet they often share one red label.

Problem 3

Managers lack context

Approvers see a fare and a reason but not the trade offs, so requests go back and forth.

How might we help employees pick a sensible flight that fits policy in a few seconds, and give their manager everything needed to approve it at a glance?
03 Approach

Four principles guided every screen.

Principle 1

Answer first, details later

Each card shows the flight, the price, whether it needs approval and its biggest catch.

Principle 2

Each risk looks different

Policy issues, trade offs, document problems and system errors each have their own icon, label, wording and colour.

Principle 3

Approval with context

Before sending, the traveller sees exactly what the manager will receive.

Principle 4

Priorities change by screen size

Desktop allows side by side comparison. Mobile shows only what the current step needs.

04 Search results

Comparison built for a wide screen.

05 Details to approval

Send once, with everything the manager needs.

06 Mobile

The same flow, rearranged for mobile.

07 Innovation

Business Trip Readiness.

Price and duration don't tell you whether an international work trip is practical. Readiness gives each itinerary a label (Excellent, Fair, Needs attention or Not travel ready) based on connections, arrival time, document checks and policy. Each label is based on clear rules and always lists its reasons.

08 States & system

When something fails, nothing is lost.

09 Decisions & learnings

Why the design looks the way it does.

The next step would be testing with travellers and approvers. I would want to know whether people trust the readiness label, and whether the approval preview really reduces the back and forth.
