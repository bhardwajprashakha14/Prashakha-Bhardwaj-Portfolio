Case study 03 Healthcare concept project

Jaanch: lab tests at home, without the guesswork

Booking a home lab test takes two minutes in most apps. The confusing part comes after. Jaanch is a concept for home sample collection in Jaipur, and I designed it around everything that happens after the booking.

My roleResearch, UX, UI, prototype and design system
TypeSelf initiated concept
PlatformsPatient app, website, ops console and collector app
Timeline4 weeks, October 2026
ToolsFigma, FigJam, Figma Make, Claude, ChatGPT

NoteThis is a concept project with no client and no real users yet, so there are no outcome numbers. The personas are assumptions I would test in interviews. About 7 minutes to read.

01 Overview

One booking, four apps.

Jaanch helps people book the tests their doctor asked for, prepare correctly, follow every test until its report is out, and recover quickly when a sample can't be used. The same booking is shared with the collector at the door and the lab team behind it.

Usability testing with patients and collectors is the next step.

Patient app

Book, prepare and track

Pick tests, see prep rules before choosing a slot, follow each test and get reports.

Patient website

The same booking on a bigger screen

The booking summary stays visible while you fill in details.

Ops console

Who needs to act

A queue for failed samples, delays and re visits, each with an owner.

Collector app

Verify, scan and hand over

Works offline at the patient's door.

02 Empathize

Booking works well. The rest needs help.

I started with desk research, a review of existing lab test apps and an assumption map. Three insights shaped the rest of the project.

Insight 1

Booking is already easy

Every big provider lets you search, book and download reports. That alone won't make anyone switch.

Insight 2

Test names are confusing

Prescriptions say TFT or FBS, or are handwritten, and patients can't match them to the app.

Insight 3

Preparation decides the result

Fasting tests need 8 to 12 hours without food, so a 6 PM slot for a fasting test doesn't work.

03 People

Two people who feel the same gap.

I made two proto personas and marked them as assumptions to test. One is a patient who books for herself and her father. The other is a coordinator who hears about failed samples too late.

"When a sample fails, I find out late, and the patient finds out even later."Anjali, collection coordinator (proto persona)
04 Define

People need to know what happens next.

Goal. Help people book the right tests, prepare with confidence and follow each test until the report is ready. When a sample has to be taken again, there should be a clear path and a person responsible for it.

How might we 1

Book the right test

Help people book the exact test without knowing medical names.

How might we 2

Make prep hard to miss

Make fasting and preparation rules impossible to overlook.

How might we 3

Share bad news calmly

Tell someone their sample failed without scaring them.

How might we 4

Own every failure

Make sure someone owns each failed sample until it is fixed.

05 Strategy

Three ideas guided every screen.

Idea 1

Show prep before the slot

If fasting decides the time, people see fasting rules first and get a reminder the evening before.

Idea 2

A status for each test

A ready blood count is shown right away, even if the thyroid test is still running.

Idea 3

Every failure has an owner

A failed sample is assigned in the ops console, and the patient books a free re visit in two taps.

06 Design system

30 components in light and dark.

Tokens for colour, type, spacing and corner radius in both themes. Every status badge has a word as well as a colour.

07 Final design

The screens, app by app.

08 Reflection

What I'd test first.

All of this is still a hypothesis until real patients and collectors try it. My test plan starts with the two riskiest questions. Can people find the right test from a prescription? And does the failed sample message feel reassuring?

The booking is the easy part. The real design work is in what comes after.
