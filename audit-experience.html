Case study 02 Enterprise OTT operator console

Redesigning the audit experience

Operators use audit logs to find out what changed and who changed it. The old module made that slow and tiring. I moved version history into the channel page and added a side by side comparison, which cut an 11 step investigation down to 6 steps.

01 Overview

From a record nobody liked opening to a tool people use.

Operators check audit logs to troubleshoot issues and stay compliant across thousands of channels, VOD assets, households and devices. The old experience made them leave what they were doing, open a separate module and piece the change together one field at a time.

I led the redesign. Version history now lives inside the channel page, and a side by side comparison highlights what was added, updated, removed or left unchanged.

02 The problem

Finding the record took longer than understanding the change.

Audit logs lived in their own module. To check one channel, an operator had to leave it, remember its Entity ID, set up the filters again and scroll through a long list. All of this happened before they saw a single changed value.

  1. Filters were the only way inThere was no link from a channel to its own history.
  2. IDs without linksOperators matched Entity IDs by hand.
  3. One record at a timeThe eye icon opened a single record, never two versions together.
  4. Everything in one listAll services and entity types were mixed together.
Problem 1

Three fields to remember

Tenant ID, Entity ID and Entity Type had to be typed in from memory before any result appeared.

Problem 2

Nothing to compare

The detail panel showed one point in time, so working out the change was left to the operator.

03 Research

Nobody starts an investigation in the audit module.

This is an internal product, so we didn't run formal usability panels. I used stakeholder conversations, support team shadowing, developer workshops and a review of how people moved between modules.

Context comes first

Operators look for history while they are already on a channel.

Finding 2

Searching was the slow part

Finding the right record took longer than understanding it.

Finding 3

One clear answer

People wanted to know what changed, not see every record.

Finding 4

The top request

Comparing two versions side by side wasn't possible at all.

Frustrations

Too many clicks, filters set up again and again, constant switching between modules.

Needs

06 Exploration

I tried four layouts. One held up with real data.

Option A, rejected

Horizontal cards

Too much sideways scrolling for channels with dozens of versions.

Option B, rejected

A modal window

Got crowded at once and had no room for a real comparison.

Option C, rejected

A separate comparison page

Operators lost their place in the channel.

Option D, chosen

History inside the channel

Kept people in context and worked for any number of versions.

Decision 1

History on the channel page

Bring the history to the operator instead of making them search.

Decision 2

Checkboxes to pick versions

Pick two versions from the list instead of typing version IDs.

Decision 3

Side by side view

People spot differences faster when two versions sit next to each other.

Decision 4

Colour and a label

Added, updated, removed and unchanged always have a word as well as a colour.

Decision 5

Metadata first

Time, service and user appear before field details, so operators trust what they are comparing.

07 Final design

History is one click away, on the same page.

  1. One clickA clear button instead of a menu item.
  2. Next to RecordingsMakes it clear the history belongs to this channel.
  3. Details stay visibleThe values being checked stay on screen.
  4. The ID comes alongNobody has to remember or copy the Channel ID.
  1. Newest firstMost investigations start with the latest change.
  2. CheckboxesThe fastest and least error prone option we tried.
  3. No error messages neededWith 0, 1 or 3 versions selected, Compare simply stays disabled.
  4. Load moreKeeps long histories fast to open.
08 The comparison screen

From reading everything to seeing the change.

AddedUpdatedRemovedUnchanged
  1. Details before changesTime, service and user for both versions come first.
  2. One legendThe colours are explained once at the top.
  3. Filter by change typeShow only removed values, or only added ones.
  4. Every languageRemoved text is struck through and new or updated text is coloured.
  5. Long text works tooDescriptions are compared the same way as single words.
  6. Badges on sectionsCollapsed sections still show whether something changed inside.

Why side by side? An inline change log was compact but hard to compare. A list of changes one after another was easy to build but needed a lot of scrolling. Side by side was the clearest and the easiest to scan.

I also designed the edge states. There are screens for no history yet, a single version, a failed comparison, a deleted version and missing permissions.

09 Before and after

The same investigation, done differently.

Before

After

10 Handoff and learnings

The work continued after Figma.

Handoff

What I shared

Annotated Figma files, every UI state, empty, loading and error states, selection rules, API mapping and edge cases.

Build

How we worked

Weekly pairing with backend and frontend engineers, then design QA before release.

Lesson 1

Context matters most

People should be able to finish a task without leaving it. Most other decisions came from this.

Lesson 2

Show, don't list

Highlighting changes made dense audit data much easier to read.

Lesson 3

Small savings add up

Removing one step matters when a task happens dozens of times a day.

What I would do next: AI summaries of changes, a visual timeline, PDF export, search by version or user, change alerts, comparing more than two versions, and comments on changes.

Operators now find the answer on the page they were already working on.
